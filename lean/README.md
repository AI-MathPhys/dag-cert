# Lean 4 formalization

Machine-checked proofs, against [Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4), of results from **Certify the Decision, Not the Outcome: Bayesian Experimental Design on Search Graphs** ([manuscript](../manuscript/Joint_cert_manuscript.pdf)).

**Status: 62 of the 62 numbered results have a proved Lean statement (0 partly proved, 0 open).** Read each row's note: many statements are proved in a specific model or with stated differences from the manuscript, summarised under "Limits of the formalization" below. Everything in this directory compiles with no `sorry` and no added axioms (the theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`).

How to read the status column:

- **proved**: the result is proved as stated, or in modular form where the only hypotheses are other numbered results of the paper, the definition of the procedure being analysed, or conditions the manuscript leaves implicit. The row says which.
- **partial**: named parts are proved and the row lists what is assumed or still open. The recurring gaps are a small set of probabilistic facts that Mathlib does not have (the change of measure for a stopped adaptive process, the self-normalized martingale bound, chi-square tails, exponential supermartingales for bounded differences) and running-time claims that need a formal cost model.
- **open**: not yet formalized.

## How statements are modelled

- A root action is its support `S : Finset Z`, nonempty, over a finite nonempty terminal type `Z`. An environment is a function `f : Z → ℝ`.
- Worst-case regret over a set `C` of environments is stated two ways: as a certificate (`∀ f ∈ C, regret S f ≤ ε ↔ …`) and as a value (`sSup`), the latter where the supremum exists.
- The ellipsoid is `m + L(B₂(β))` for a linear map `L` between finite-dimensional real inner product spaces; `Lᵀ` is `LinearMap.adjoint L`. No rank condition is assumed.
- The manuscript proves the minimax identity by Sion's theorem. Here it is proved from the Hahn-Banach separation theorem in Mathlib.
- Algorithms are not modelled as programs. A run is a sequence of selections satisfying the rule the paper states (for example "the selected index maximises the PUCT score"), so the theorems hold for every tie-breaking rule. Stopping monitors enter through their specification.
- Probabilistic statements are over an arbitrary probability space carrying the reward tapes, and are stated as bounds on the failure probability.
- Asymptotic statements (`Θ`, `O`, `Õ`) are proved as explicit two-sided inequalities with named constants.
- Running-time claims use an explicit step-counting model (`CostModel.lean`): a program returns its result together with a cost computed by the same recursion. Indexed heaps are a primitive charged `⌈log₂ m⌉ + 1` per key update.
- Samplers in the lower-bound results are deterministic functions of the observed history; probabilities and expectations for unbounded runs are limits over a finite horizon.

## Hypotheses the manuscript leaves implicit

Found while formalizing; none makes a stated result false.

- `thm:active-restart` uses `ρ_z ≤ 1` and `M ≥ |Z|`.
- `cor:v2countscales`: the shrinking-gap lower bound is derived from a count bound that needs `t ≥ 2K`.
- Safe permanent retirement preserves the stopping bit only for `ε ≥ 0`.
- `thm:active-star-lb` needs the return probability to be positive under interior alternatives.
- `prop:ast-gap-tradeoff` needs `N ≥ 1`.
- The Safeguarded-LCB width argument needs the lower-confidence candidate to maximise the lower endpoint over the support.
- The empirical-Bernstein betting parameter needs `δ < 2M`.
- `thm:t4-finite-sample` needs `δ < 1`; `cor:v35poly` is proved for `ε ≤ 1/e`.
- `prop:representation-cost`: "each gate endpoint changes at most once per wave" needs gates to be processed in topological order; an arbitrary delivery order can change a gate twice, and the scheduling cost does not appear in the stated bound.
- `lem:v34-batch-comparison` needs the null set to be nonempty; the second term of `eq:v34-explicit-tail` uses that the tail is nonincreasing in the batch size, since batches have random size at least `b_i`.
- `thm:v34-adaptive`: the proof's union bound combines per-query intrinsic tapes with conditionally fresh batches; on intrinsic tapes the batch term needs either a conditional argument or an extra factor counting segments.
- The "alternating ladder" in the last paragraph of the proof of `thm:onpolicy-uct` is never defined.
- `thm:v34-adaptive` needs the numerical oracles to be Borel measurable.
- The UCT index is written with `log N_t(v)` in its definition but used with `log(1+t)` (and coefficient 2 in one place) in the proofs of the funnel and common-core theorems.

## Limits of the formalization

Every numbered result has a proved Lean statement, but several statements differ from a literal reading of the manuscript. The differences are listed in each row; the recurring ones are:

- **Algorithms are specified, not programmed.** A run of a sampler or search is any sequence satisfying the stated selection rule. The tracking procedures are explicit, but their numerical oracles are taken through the contract the paper stipulates, shown satisfiable by existence arguments rather than by the paper's finite grids and rational arithmetic. Stopping rules use exact statistics, not verified numerical enclosures.
- **Samplers in lower bounds are deterministic** functions of the observed history; internal randomisation is not modelled. Probabilities and expectations for unbounded runs are limits over a finite horizon, except where a concrete tape space is constructed (restart bound, Gaussian tracker).
- **Asymptotic statements are explicit inequalities** with named constants, or limits in eventual form. Some constants differ from the displayed ones (for example the discovery constant of `thm:v34-adaptive`).
- **Running-time claims hold in a step-counting model** in which a program returns its result with a cost computed by the same recursion. Heap operations are executed by an implemented binary heap in the final cost theorems; scans, list unlinks, bucket operations and some initialisations are unit or per-element charges by definition. Bounds for the DAG engines carry a level-scheduling term that the manuscript's bounds do not show.
- **Some statements are restricted.** The work separations use tree-shaped circuits with root retirement; `thm:bay-main-optimal` assumes every action has a strict alternative; `thm:coded-main` models the eager monitor only; `cor:v35poly` does not compose its two clauses into one statement.
- **Review.** Each file compiles and the axiom audit is mechanical, but the Lean statements were written by an automated assistant, and their correspondence with the manuscript's wording has not been independently reviewed. Rows marked proved should be read together with their notes.
